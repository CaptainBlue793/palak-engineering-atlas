Extractive Question Answering

Extractive QA answers a question by pointing at a span of a given passage: “When was the tower completed?” → “1889”, copied from the text. It is the reading half of search engines, help desks and the retrieval-augmented systems that feed LLMs, and it is a clean way to see what an encoder actually reads.

This chapter fine-tunes DistilBERT on SQuAD 1.1, scores it with exact match and F1 written from scratch, and then tests what the model really relies on. Fine-tuned for 8 minutes, DistilBERT reached 75.98 exact match and 84.54 F1 on SQuAD 1.1. Then the probes: given only the first word of each question (“When”, “Who”), it still scored 16.49 F1, and when the sentence containing the answer was deleted it answered every single question anyway. A SQuAD 2.0 model trained to abstain declined 76% of those.

  • Frame QA as predicting a start and an end token
  • Handle passages longer than the model with sliding windows
  • Train for unanswerable questions and set a no-answer threshold
  • Compute SQuAD exact match and F1 by hand
  • Probe what a QA model exploits instead of reading

Span start and end

Feed the encoder “[CLS] question [SEP] passage [SEP]”. A linear layer gives every token two scores, one for being the answer’s first token and one for being its last; training is cross-entropy on the gold start and end positions. At prediction time, take the pair (start, end) with the highest combined score such that start ≤ end, the span is at most 30 tokens long, and both lie inside the passage, then map the tokens back to characters with the tokeniser’s offsets.

out = model(input_ids=x, attention_mask=mask)                          # AutoModelForQuestionAnswering
loss = (F.cross_entropy(out.start_logits, start) + F.cross_entropy(out.end_logits, end)) / 2
# decode: best s ≤ e with e − s < 30, both in the passage, maximising start_logits[s] + end_logits[e]
answer = context[offsets[s][0]:offsets[e][1]]

Sliding windows over long passages

A passage plus a question can exceed the model’s window (384 tokens here). The tokeniser then cuts the passage into overlapping windows (return_overflowing_tokens=True, stride=128), each paired with the same question. In training, a window that does not contain the answer is labelled with start = end = 0, the [CLS] position. At prediction, every window proposes its best span and the highest-scoring one across windows wins.

Exact match and F1

SQuAD scores a prediction against every gold answer (validation questions have up to five, from different annotators) and keeps the best. Both scores first normalise: lower-case, remove punctuation and the articles a, an, the, collapse spaces. Exact match is 1 if the normalised strings are identical. F1 treats both as bags of tokens and computes precision and recall of the overlap, so “in 1889” against “1889” gets partial credit. Try spans in the lab.

The 87,599 training questions became 88,524 windows of 384 tokens; only 739 of them lacked their answer, because SQuAD passages are short. Two epochs took 8 minutes and reached 82.73 F1 after the first and 84.54 after the second (75.98 exact match), close to the 85.8 F1 reported in the DistilBERT paper. F1 did not fall with passage length (84.1 for 0-128 tokens, 84.57 for 128-256 tokens, 86.08 for 256-384 tokens, 85.51 for 384-10000 tokens): the windows did their job.

Unanswerable questions

SQuAD 1.1 always has an answer in the passage, so a model trained on it always answers. SQuAD 2.0 adds over 50,000 questions written to look answerable but with no answer in the passage; models learn to put the probability at [CLS] instead, and the system answers only when the best span’s score beats [CLS]’s by a threshold. On its 11,873 validation questions (5,945 unanswerable), deepset/roberta-base-squad2 at threshold 0 scored 82.93 F1 overall: 84.1 on the answerable questions and 81.77% correct abstentions on the others. Raising the threshold to 6 made it abstain correctly 94.11% of the time, but F1 on answerable questions fell to 62.41; lowering it to −4 did the reverse (88.68 and 68.73%). The best overall F1 here was at 2 (83.27). The threshold is a product decision, not a model property.

What QA models exploit

Two probes, both on SQuAD 1.1 validation with the model trained here. First, cut every question to its first word. F1 fell from 84.54 to 16.49, not to zero: “When” alone is enough to pick a date-like span, “Who” a name. The answers show the pattern: “Which” → “Super Bowl L” (gold “Denver Broncos”); “Who” → “Coldplay” (gold “Beyoncé and Bruno Mars”). Second, delete the sentence that contains the answer from 8,524 passages. The SQuAD 1.1 model answered 100% of them anyway, at 5.31 F1: it has no way to say “not here”. The SQuAD 2.0 model abstained on 76% of the same questions. Jia and Liang (2017) found the related weakness in the other direction: add a distracting sentence that shares words with the question and SQuAD models jump to it.

Use case: answering questions from product manuals

A manufacturer lets technicians ask questions of 4,000 pages of manuals. A retriever finds the three most relevant passages (Chapter 26) and an extractive QA model points at the answer, shown with its surrounding sentence so the technician can check it. The team learns the three lessons of this chapter. Long manual sections need sliding windows, or answers near the end are never seen. The SQuAD-1 model always answers, so they switch to a SQuAD-2 model and set the no-answer threshold on 300 real questions, choosing a point where wrong answers are rare even though “no answer found” becomes common. And they check what the model is matching: questions that copy a phrase from the wrong section get confidently wrong answers, so the retriever’s quality matters more than the reader’s.

⚠️
Common mistakes
  • Deploying a SQuAD 1.1 model where answers may be missing: with the answer sentence deleted it still answered 100% of questions.
  • Cutting long passages into non-overlapping chunks: 128-token windows without overlap cost 2.6 F1; use a stride.
  • Leaving the no-answer threshold at 0 without looking: between −4 and 6 the share of correct abstentions ran from 68.73% to 94.11%.
  • Scoring against one gold answer: SQuAD has several; score against all and keep the best.
  • Decoding spans that cross into the question or end before they start: restrict to the passage and start ≤ end.
  • Reading 90 F1 as “understands the passage”: the probes below show how much is pattern matching.

Interview drill

Explain it simply: How does an extractive QA model find an answer?

How a strong answer sounds
The question and passage go into the encoder together, “[CLS] question [SEP] passage [SEP]”, so every passage token is encoded in light of the question. A linear layer gives each token a start score and an end score; the answer is the span with the highest start + end score where start ≤ end, the span is short, and both ends are inside the passage. Training is cross-entropy on the gold start and end positions. Passages longer than the window are cut into overlapping windows and the best span across windows wins. A DistilBERT fine-tuned this way on SQuAD 1.1 reached 84.54 F1 in 8 minutes. The model only points at text; it cannot generate or combine facts that are not written in one span.

Deeper: Your QA system gives confident answers when the passage does not contain one. How do you fix it?

How a strong answer sounds
A model trained only on answerable questions has learnt that some span always answers, so it never abstains: here, with the answer sentence deleted, a SQuAD 1.1 model answered 100% of questions. The fix is to train on unanswerable examples too, as SQuAD 2.0 does, so the model learns to put its probability on [CLS], and then to answer only when the best span beats the no-answer score by a threshold. I would set that threshold on a few hundred real questions from the product, choosing the point where wrong answers are rare enough; for the SQuAD 2.0 model I measured, moving it from 0 to 6 raised correct abstentions from 81.77% to 94.11% while answerable F1 fell from 84.1 to 62.41. In a retrieval pipeline, the retriever’s scores give a second signal: if no passage is relevant, do not ask the reader at all.

🧠 Key takeaways

  • Extractive QA predicts a start and an end token in the passage, decoded as the best valid span.
  • Measured: DistilBERT fine-tuned on SQuAD 1.1 reached 75.98 EM and 84.54 F1; 128-token windows without overlap cost 2.6 F1.
  • Measured: a SQuAD 1.1 model answered 100% of questions whose answer had been deleted; a SQuAD 2.0 model abstained on 76%, with the threshold trading abstentions against answers.
  • Measured: one-word questions (“When”, “Who”) still scored 16.49 F1: answer-type matching does part of the work.
  • Exact match and F1 normalise text and take the best match over all gold answers.
