Two-Stage Detectors: Faster R-CNN

A two-stage detector first asks “where might there be an object?” and only then “what is it, exactly where?”. The first stage, a region proposal network, turns a few hundred thousand anchors into about a thousand candidate regions. The second crops features for each region and classifies and refines it. Faster R-CNN, published in 2015 and still a standard baseline, is the clearest example.

This chapter traces one COCO image through torchvision’s Faster R-CNN, with the tensor shape and the time of every stage: 217,413 anchors, 1,000 proposals, 1,000 cropped 7 × 7 feature grids, 6 detections. It measures why RoI Align replaced RoI Pool (21.6 pixels of misplacement against none), compares three variants on 500 COCO images, and fine-tunes the detector on 120 photos of pedestrians.

From R-CNN to Faster R-CNN

R-CNN (2014) took about 2,000 region proposals from a classical algorithm (selective search), warped each crop to 227 × 227 and ran a whole CNN on every one: tens of seconds per image. Fast R-CNN (2015) ran the CNN once on the whole image and cropped each region from the feature map instead (RoI pooling), so the expensive part was shared. Faster R-CNN (2015) replaced selective search with a region proposal network that shares the same features, making the whole detector one network trained end to end. The later FPN version adds a feature pyramid so small and large objects are each handled at a suitable resolution.

Tracing one image

m = fasterrcnn_resnet50_fpn_v2(weights=FasterRCNN_ResNet50_FPN_V2_Weights.COCO_V1).cuda().eval()
il, _ = m.transform([img])                     # 375x500 -> resized 800x1066, padded to 800x1088 (multiple of 32)
feats = m.backbone(il.tensors)                 # OrderedDict: '0' (256,200,272) ... 'pool' (256,13,17)
props, _ = m.rpn(il, feats)                    # [ (1000, 4) ]: proposals after NMS
pooled = m.roi_heads.box_roi_pool(feats, props, il.image_sizes)   # (1000, 256, 7, 7)
dets, _ = m.roi_heads(feats, props, il.image_sizes)              # boxes, labels, scores

The step-through in the panel shows each stage’s output for a 500 × 375 COCO photo. Two things are worth noticing. The RPN scores 217,413 anchors (three aspect ratios at every position of five pyramid levels) but passes on only 1,000 proposals; the second stage’s cost is fixed by that number, not by the image. And the box head works on 1,000 small 7 × 7 grids, about 50 million values, regardless of what is in the photo: two-stage detectors spend the same effort on an empty street as on a crowded one.

Feature pyramids: which level for which box

A feature pyramid network takes the backbone’s stages at strides 4, 8, 16 and 32 and adds a top-down path: each coarse, semantically strong map is upsampled and added to the finer map below it, so every level has both detail and meaning. A region is then pooled from the level whose stride suits its size, by k = ⌊4 + log₂(√(w·h) / 224)⌋, clamped to levels 2–5: a 224-pixel region from P4 (stride 16), a 56-pixel one from P2 (stride 4). For the 1,000 proposals of the traced image: 551 from P2, 226 from P3, 183 from P4 and 40 from P5. Most proposals are small, and small objects get the high-resolution map they need.

RoI Pool vs RoI Align

To crop a region’s features, the original RoI Pool rounded the box to the feature grid, divided it into 7 × 7 bins with rounded edges, and max-pooled each bin. Each rounding can shift the crop by up to one feature cell: at stride 16, sixteen pixels of the input. RoI Align (from Mask R-CNN, 2017) keeps the box’s exact fractional coordinates and samples each bin with bilinear interpolation at regular points. To measure the difference, both were run on a feature map whose value is its own x-coordinate, for 500 random boxes at stride 16, comparing each bin’s output with the true x-coordinate of the bin’s centre:

MethodMean error per bin, feature cellsin input pixels (×16)
RoI Pool (max over quantised bins)1.34821.6 px
RoI Align (bilinear, 2 × 2 samples per bin, aligned)0.0000.0 px

On a linear feature map bilinear sampling is exact, so RoI Align’s error is zero; RoI Pool is off by more than a feature cell on average (part of that is the max, which picks each bin’s right edge). Twenty pixels barely matters for a box around a car; it matters a great deal for a 28 × 28 mask (Chapter 18), which is why Mask R-CNN introduced RoI Align, and why every modern two-stage detector uses it.

Where the time goes, and three variants

Median time per stage over 100 COCO images, one image at a time: transform 0.16 ms, backbone + FPN 13.6 ms, RPN 8.1 ms, RoI heads 9.6 ms, post-processing 0.13 ms, about 32 ms in total. The backbone is under half of it; the proposal network and the per-region head, the “two-stage” overhead, are the rest.

The v2 model is the same design retrained with a better recipe and a few architectural tweaks (normalisation in the FPN and heads, a deeper box head), and gained about ten AP points: the same lesson as Chapter 11. The MobileNet backbone made the detector three times faster and lost most on small objects (15.7 against 33.0), because its features at high resolution are weaker.

Fine-tuning a detector

To detect a new set of classes, replace the box predictor (the final classification and regression layer) and fine-tune. On the Penn-Fudan pedestrian dataset, 170 photos of people in the street, with 120 for training: starting from the COCO detector, AP50 on the 50 held-out photos reached 99.4 after one epoch and 99.8 after ten. Starting from only an ImageNet backbone, with the FPN, RPN and heads trained from scratch on 120 photos, it reached 46.1 after ten epochs. The COCO detector already knew people; the ImageNet backbone knew images, and 120 photos are far too few to learn proposals and box regression from scratch. Detection is even more dependent on pretraining than classification (Chapter 8).

from torchvision.models.detection.faster_rcnn import FastRCNNPredictor
m = fasterrcnn_resnet50_fpn_v2(weights=FasterRCNN_ResNet50_FPN_V2_Weights.COCO_V1)
m.roi_heads.box_predictor = FastRCNNPredictor(1024, num_classes=2)    # background + pedestrian
# training: m(images, targets) returns a dict of four losses (RPN objectness, RPN boxes, classifier, box regression)
losses = m([img], [{"boxes": boxes, "labels": labels}]); sum(losses.values()).backward()

Use case: when two-stage detectors are still the choice

For many offline and accuracy-first jobs (counting cells in microscopy, finding defects on scanned circuit boards, cataloguing products on shelf photos, annotation tools that pre-draw boxes for human labellers) the extra 10–20 ms of a two-stage detector is irrelevant and its accuracy on small, crowded objects is what matters. Two-stage detectors also extend naturally: Mask R-CNN adds a mask head, Keypoint R-CNN a keypoint head (Chapter 18), each reading the same RoI-aligned features. For real-time video on modest hardware, one-stage detectors are usually preferred (Chapter 15).

⚠️
Common mistakes
  • Using RoI Pool (or any quantised crop) where alignment matters, such as masks.
  • Forgetting the internal resize: torchvision detectors resize to an 800-pixel shorter side, so input resolution and speed depend on that setting, not your image.
  • Training the detector heads from scratch on a small dataset instead of starting from a COCO detector (46 vs 99 AP50 here).
  • Changing the number of classes without replacing the box predictor, or forgetting that class 0 is background.
  • Comparing AP measured on a subset with published full-set numbers.

Interview drill

Explain it simply: Walk through Faster R-CNN from image to boxes.

How a strong answer sounds
The image is resized (800-pixel shorter side in torchvision) and passed through a backbone with a feature pyramid, giving maps at strides 4 to 64. The region proposal network slides over every level and, for each anchor (here 217,413 for one image), predicts an objectness score and box offsets; the top boxes are decoded, de-duplicated with NMS, and about 1,000 proposals are kept. For each proposal, RoI Align crops a 7 × 7 grid of features from the pyramid level that suits its size, and the second-stage head classifies it into the 91 COCO categories and refines the box; a final per-class NMS and score threshold give the detections. Training uses four losses: objectness and box regression for the RPN, classification and box regression for the head. On this GPU the backbone took about 14 ms of the 32 ms, the RPN 8 and the heads 10.

Deeper: Why did Mask R-CNN need RoI Align, and how big is the error it removes?

How a strong answer sounds
RoI Pool rounds the box to integer feature-map coordinates and rounds each of the 7 × 7 bin boundaries again, so the crop can be misaligned by up to a feature cell, sixteen input pixels at stride 16. For classification that hardly matters, but a mask head predicts pixel-accurate shapes from those features, so misalignment shows up directly as masks offset from the object. RoI Align keeps fractional coordinates and bilinearly samples a few regular points per bin. Measured on a feature map whose value equals its x-coordinate, RoI Pool’s bins were off by 1.35 cells (about 21.6 input pixels) on average, while RoI Align was exact. Mask R-CNN reported large mask AP gains from this one change, especially at strict IoU thresholds.

🧠 Key takeaways

  • Stage 1 (RPN) turns 217,413 anchors into 1,000 class-agnostic proposals; stage 2 classifies and refines each one.
  • FPN gives every level detail and meaning; regions are pooled from the level matching their size (551 of 1,000 from P2 here).
  • RoI Align samples exact fractional positions: 0 px error against RoI Pool’s 21.6 px.
  • Of 32 ms per image, 14 went to the backbone and 18 to the two-stage machinery.
  • The v2 recipe added about 10 AP; a MobileNet backbone was 3× faster and lost most on small objects.
  • Fine-tuning from a COCO detector reached 99.4 AP50 on pedestrians in one epoch; from an ImageNet backbone only, 46.1 after ten.

Check your understanding

What does Faster R-CNN’s region proposal network output?

It scores every anchor as object or background, refines the boxes, applies NMS and keeps the top proposals for the second stage.

From which FPN level is a 56 × 56-pixel region pooled?

k = ⌊4 + log₂(56/224)⌋ = ⌊4 − 2⌋ = 2. Small regions use the finest map.

Why does RoI Align beat RoI Pool?

Measured: 21.6 px mean misplacement for RoI Pool, 0.0 for RoI Align on a linear feature map.

Where did most of Faster R-CNN v2’s 32 ms per image go?

Backbone + FPN 13.6 ms, RPN 8.1, RoI heads 9.6: the two-stage machinery cost more than the backbone.

Fine-tuned on 120 pedestrian photos, which start gave 99% AP50 after one epoch?

The COCO detector already detects people. From an ImageNet backbone only, it reached 46.1 after ten epochs.
