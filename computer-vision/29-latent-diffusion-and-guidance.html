Latent Diffusion, Samplers & Guidance

The diffusion model of Chapter 28 worked on 32 × 32 pixels. Run the same process on 512 × 512 images and every one of its many steps costs a U-Net pass over 786,432 numbers. Latent diffusion (2022), the idea behind Stable Diffusion, first compresses the image with a VAE into a much smaller grid of latents, runs diffusion there, and decodes once at the end. Text enters through cross-attention, and a trick called classifier-free guidance decides how literally the image follows it.

This chapter takes Stable Diffusion 1.5 apart on one GPU: what the VAE keeps and loses, where the time goes, and what the guidance scale and step count do to quality, measured with CLIP score. One 512 × 512 image took 1.3 seconds, 95% of it in the U-Net. The VAE compressed a photo 48 times and decoded it back at 31.2 dB. Raising the guidance scale from 1 to 20 raised CLIP score from 28.2 to 33.2, with most of the gain by 2, while the images went from incoherent to over-saturated.

Diffusing in latent space

Most of the numbers in a 512 × 512 photo encode fine texture that is easy to reconstruct and costly to generate step by step. Stable Diffusion’s VAE maps the image to a 64 × 64 × 4 latent, 8 times smaller per side, then decodes back. Its KL weight is tiny, so the latent behaves like a compressed image rather than a sample from N(0, 1), and its decoder was trained with a perceptual and an adversarial loss as well as pixel error, so reconstructions stay sharp: the opposite trade-off to Chapter 26’s VAEs. Diffusion then runs entirely on the latents, with the U-Net seeing 16,384 positions instead of 262,144 pixels.

Encoding the 512 × 512 cat photo to its 64 × 64 × 4 latent and decoding it again gave 31.2 dB PSNR: at a glance the two images are indistinguishable (the panel’s third view). That is 48 times fewer numbers with less error than Chapter 26’s best CIFAR autoencoder achieved at 6 times compression (26.7 dB, on very different images, so only a rough comparison), the payoff of a decoder trained with perceptual and adversarial losses on millions of images. The VAE has 83.7 M parameters; the U-Net that does the diffusion, 859.5 M; the CLIP text encoder, 123.1 M.

Text conditioning

The prompt is tokenised and passed through CLIP’s text encoder (ViT-L/14’s, frozen), giving 77 token vectors of 768 numbers. Inside the U-Net, many blocks contain a cross-attention layer: the queries come from the image latents, and the keys and values from the text tokens, so each spatial position can pull in whichever words are relevant to it. That is how “red” ends up on the bicycle rather than the wall: positions that look like a bicycle attend to the tokens “red bicycle”.

Classifier-free guidance

A conditional diffusion model trained on captions follows them only loosely: many captions are vague, and the model learns to hedge. Classifier-free guidance (2021) sharpens it at sampling time. During training the caption is dropped (replaced by an empty prompt) for about 10% of examples, so the same network learns both a conditional prediction ε(xt, text) and an unconditional one ε(xt, ∅). At each sampling step both are computed, and the prediction is pushed further in the direction the text moves it:

ε̂ = ε(xt, ∅) + s · ( ε(xt, text) − ε(xt, ∅) )

Scale s = 1 is plain conditional sampling; s > 1 exaggerates whatever distinguishes “this prompt” from “any image”. It costs two U-Net evaluations per step (usually run as one batch of two).

e_uncond, e_text = unet(torch.cat([x, x]), t, encoder_hidden_states=torch.cat([emb_empty, emb_prompt])).chunk(2)
eps = e_uncond + scale * (e_text - e_uncond)                           # classifier-free guidance
x = scheduler.step(eps, t, x).prev_sample                              # DDIM update from Chapter 28

Without guidance (s = 1) the model produced dreamlike, half-formed scenes: a bicycle fused into a wall, a bowl full of shapeless texture. Most of the gain in prompt alignment came by s = 2; beyond that CLIP score kept creeping up, to 33.2 at s = 20, while the pictures became over-saturated and more cartoon-like (the red wall and the robot in the panel). CLIP score measures alignment, not naturalness, so it rewarded this. My diversity proxy, the pixel difference between two seeds’ images, did not show the expected loss of variety: it rose with guidance, because more contrasty images differ more pixel by pixel even when they are more alike in content. A crude pixel metric cannot measure diversity; Chapter 30’s feature-space recall is the right tool. Eight images per scale is also a small sample, so read the trend, not the decimals.

Steps and samplers

Because the U-Net dominates the cost, the number of sampling steps is the main dial between speed and quality. Stable Diffusion was trained with 1,000 noise levels, and DDIM (Chapter 28) or better solvers such as DPM-Solver++ sample from it in 20–50 steps.

Time scaled linearly with steps, 0.27 to 2.18 seconds per image. CLIP score barely moved and not monotonically (with four prompts, differences under about a point are noise), because the composition that CLIP judges is fixed in the first few steps; what more steps buy is texture and sharpness, visible at 5 steps as softness. For production that is the reason to use 20–30 steps with a good solver, or a distilled few-step model, rather than 50.

The cost of one image

Timed component by component for one image at 30 steps and s = 7.5: the text encoder ran once in 9.1 ms, each U-Net step (batch of two for guidance) took 41.3 ms, so 30 steps cost 1.24 s, and the VAE decoder ran once in 51.2 ms. End to end through the pipeline: 1.31 s, with 2.6 GB of GPU memory at fp16. The U-Net was 95% of the time. Halving the steps nearly halves the cost; dropping guidance (s = 1) would halve each step but, as measured above, ruin the images; and the VAE, which is the reason latent diffusion is affordable at all, costs less than 4%.

Use case: product photography and the guidance knob

E-commerce teams use latent diffusion to put a product into new scenes: the product photo is encoded, its region kept fixed (inpainting) and the background generated from a prompt such as “on a marble kitchen counter, morning light”. The two dials measured here are exactly what such tools expose. Guidance scale sets how literally the scene follows the prompt; too high and images look over-saturated and samey, too low and the prompt is ignored, so products usually fix it around 5–8, where, in the images here, prompt alignment has mostly been gained and colours are still natural. Step count sets cost per image; a team generating a million images a month chooses between 20-step sampling and a distilled 4-step model on the basis of a GPU-seconds bill, checked with sample metrics like those in Chapter 30.

⚠️
Common mistakes
  • Forgetting the VAE scaling factor (0.18215 for SD 1.5): latents must be scaled before diffusion and unscaled before decoding.
  • Cranking guidance up for “better” images: CLIP score kept rising up to s = 20 here, but the images became over-saturated; judge by eye and by distribution metrics, not alignment alone.
  • Comparing step counts across schedulers as if they were equal: 20 DDIM steps and 20 Euler or DPM-Solver steps differ.
  • Generating at a resolution the model was not trained on (SD 1.5: 512 px) and getting duplicated subjects.
  • Reading CLIP score as image quality: it measures text alignment, and is fooled by text drawn in the image (Chapter 24).

Interview drill

Explain it simply: What does classifier-free guidance do?

How a strong answer sounds
The model is trained to denoise both with the caption and, for a fraction of examples, with an empty caption, so one network gives a conditional and an unconditional noise prediction. At each sampling step you compute both and move past the conditional one: unconditional plus s times the difference. The difference is the direction the text pushes the image, so a scale above 1 exaggerates it, making images follow the prompt more closely at the cost of diversity and, at high scales, saturated, over-contrasted images. It doubles the U-Net work per step. I swept it on Stable Diffusion 1.5: at s = 1 images were incoherent, by s = 2 most of the prompt alignment was there, and at 20 CLIP score was highest (33.2 against 28.2) but colours were over-saturated, so I would pick around 5–8 and check with people.

Deeper: Why does Stable Diffusion diffuse in a latent space, and what does that cost?

How a strong answer sounds
Pixel-space diffusion at 512 × 512 runs a large U-Net over 786k numbers at every one of tens of steps. A VAE compresses the image 48 times to 64 × 64 × 4 latents that keep the perceptually important structure, so every step is far cheaper, and the decoder, trained with perceptual and adversarial losses, restores fine texture once at the end. The costs: anything the VAE cannot represent cannot be generated, which shows up in small text, faces at a distance and fine patterns; and the decoder adds a fixed cost per image. Measured on Stable Diffusion 1.5 at 512 px, the VAE compresses 48 times and reconstructs at 31.2 dB, and the U-Net is 95% of the 1.3-second cost of a 30-step image.

🧠 Key takeaways

  • Latent diffusion runs the diffusion process on a VAE’s 64 × 64 × 4 latent instead of 512 × 512 × 3 pixels.
  • Text conditions the U-Net through cross-attention to CLIP text-encoder tokens.
  • Classifier-free guidance: ε̂ = ε(∅) + s(ε(text) − ε(∅)); it costs two U-Net evaluations per step.
  • Measured: 1.31 s per 512 px image at 30 steps, 95% in the U-Net; the VAE round-trip was 31.2 dB at 48× compression.
  • Guidance s 1 → 2 gave most of the CLIP-score gain (28.2 → 31.5); higher scales over-saturate. Steps set cost linearly (0.27–2.18 s) and mainly add texture.

Check your understanding

What shape is Stable Diffusion 1.5’s latent for a 512 × 512 image?

8× smaller per side with 4 channels: 48 times fewer numbers than the image. 77 × 768 is the text encoding.

How does the prompt influence the U-Net?

Each spatial position attends to the words relevant to it.

With classifier-free guidance at scale s, what is the predicted noise?

Start from the unconditional prediction and move s times along the direction the text adds.

What does raising the guidance scale typically trade away?

High scales push towards the most prompt-typical image, so seeds converge and colours oversaturate. The cost per step is the same for any s > 1.

Which component dominates Stable Diffusion’s time per image?

The text encoder and VAE decoder run once; the U-Net runs tens of times.
